Abstract
Hybrid density functional calculations are used to calculate the spin density distribution and the 17O and 13C hyperfine
coupling constants for the ubisemiquinone anion radical in model complexes simulating the radical's environment in alcohol
solvents and in the Q binding site of Rb sphaeroides . Good agreement is observed between the alcohol solvent model
a
calculated couplings and experimental determinations. For the Q site model, best agreement is observed for a Q site modelaa
containing a positively charged imidazole hydrogen bonding interaction at the O4 atom of the semiquinone. This may
suggest that the asymmetry observed for the Q site of Rb sphaeroides is due to strong hydrogen bonding by the O4 oxygena
atom of the semiquinone to an imidazole carrying a partial positive charge. The obvious candidate to fulfil this role in the
.reaction centre is the HIS M219 ligand of an Fe II complex. q 1998 Elsevier Science B.V.
1. Introduction
Quinones are ubiquitous to living systems and
represent important cofactors for electron transfer in
wxphotosynthesis and respiration 1 . In photosynthesis,
for example, quinones act as electron acceptors in
the initial charge separation. For both bacterial and
higher plant Photosystem 2, two quinones termed Q
a
and Q act in concert to enable efficient chargeb
wxseparation to take place 2 . Q is initially reduced toa
form the semiquinone anion radical. This then for-
wards its electron to Q forming the Q semiquinonebb
anion radical. On further charge separation Q ac-a
cepts another electron to form the semiquinone anion
radical again. This electron is then passed on to the
already reduced Q which accepts two protons to
b
form quinol QH and leaves the protein site to be2
replaced by another quinone molecule from a quinone
pool nearby.
Q and Q are often identical quinones; plasto-
ab
quinone in higher plants and ubiquinone in bacterial
systems. A variety of spectroscopic methods most
notably, EPR, ENDOR, FTIR and NMR have been
used to investigate differences between the Q and
a
wxQ ubiquinones 3±5 . Differences in the hydrogenb
bonding ability of both ubiquinones is generally put
forward for the differing functions observed, i.e.
specific hydrogen bonds to nearby amino acid
residues are able to tailor the quinone to perform a
specific function. EPR and ENDOR studies have
afforded a detailed look at the electronic structure of
the semiquinone anion radical formed in the Q and
a
Q sites. Differences in the nature of the hydrogenb
bonding interactions of both semiquinones have been

3. Results and discussion
The spin density surfaces in Fig. 2 qualitatively
show the effect of hydrogen bonding on the spin
density distribution. For the alcohol complex Fig.
.2a a symmetrical spin density distribution is ob-
served. For the reaction centre site model A Fig.
.2b , a symmetrical spin density distribution is also
.predicted while for model B Fig. 2c an asymmetri-
cal spin density distribution is predicted. We note
that this pronounced asymmetrical spin distribution
is brought about by having a positive charge on the
imadazole hydrogen bonding residue. In the reaction
.centre a non-heme Fe II ion is the ligand to the
non-hydrogen bonded imadazole nitrogen atom of
.HIS M219. For the ENDOR studies of Q this Fe II
a
. .is replaced by Zn II . The Fe II is also ligated by
three other histidines and a glutamic acid bidentate
ligand. Preliminary PM3 calculations in this labora-
.tory on a Zn II complex containing four imidazole
and one formate ligand suggests that the overall
positive charge on the imidazole ligand is 0.5 e. As
such model B overestimates the positive charge on
the hydrogen bonding imidazole ligand but by com-
paring with model A gives us an indication of the
effect of this charge on the semiquinone radical spin
density distribution. The effect is to strengthen the
hydrogen bonding ability of the NH of the imadazole
leading to a stronger hydrogen bond at the O4
oxygen atom. The optimised geometries for the com-
.plexes of Fig. 1 Table 1 show that the O4 hydrogen
bond is considerably shorter than the O1 hydrogen
bond in the model B reaction centre site. These data
indicate that on reduction of Q to form the
a
semiquinone anion radical the ubisemiquinone anion
moves down in the site to more closely associate
.with the HIS M219±Fe II complex, thereby creating
the asymmetry in the spin density distribution ob-
served.
