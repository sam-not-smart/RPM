Pulsed ENDOR of the photoexcited triplet states of bacteriochlorophylla
and of the primary donor P865 in reaction centers of Rhodobacter
sphaeroides R-26
Friedhelm Lendzian, Robert Bittl & Wolfgang Lubitz
Max-Volmer-Institut für Biophysikalische Chemie und Biochemie, Technische Universität Berlin, Str. d. 17. Juni
135, D-10623 Berlin, Germany
Received 11 September 1997; accepted in revised form 30 January 1998
Key words: bacterial reaction center, charge transfer, excitation delocalization, ENDOR, primary donor, triplet
states
Abstract
The photoexcited triplet states of bacteriochlorophyll a, 3BChl a, and of the primary donor in reaction centers
of Rhodobacter sphaeroides R-26, 3P865, are investigated by pulsed EPR and ENDOR spectroscopy. In 3P865 a
splitting of ENDOR lines and reduction of corresponding positive and negative hyperﬁne couplings as compared
with the monomeric
3BChl a is observed. This indicates an asymmetric distribution of the triplet excitation over
the two BChl a moieties, P L and PM, forming 3P865. Based on the signs of the hyperﬁne couplings and on a
comparison with the cation and anion radical of BChl a an assignment to nuclei in the different dimer halves is
proposed. This yields an estimate for the extent of delocalization of the triplet excitation over P L and PM and for
the charge transfer contribution of 3P865.
Introduction
The electron transfer processes in photosynthetic re-
action centers (RCs) are controlled both by the spatial
arrangement of the cofactors and by their electronic
properties. The latter are determined by the wave func-
tions and orbital energies of the respective states. In
the primary processes the electron donor (P) is of
particular importance, since it initiates from its ex-
cited singlet state the electron transfer (ET) which
proceeds via monomeric bacteriochlorophyll a (BChl
a), bacteriopheophytin (BPh a) to the primary and
secondary quinone acceptors Q
A and Q B. The elec-
tron spin density distribution of the highest occupied
molecular orbital (HOMO) of the cation radical of
the primary donor, P+.
865, which remains after dona-
tion of an electron to the chain of acceptor molecules,
has been investigated in great detail by cw-EPR and
ENDOR on RC single crystals of Rhodobacter (Rb.)
sphaeroides (Lendzian et al. 1993; Huber et al. 1996).
In this work an asymmetric spin density distribution
over the two BChl a (see Scheme 1) molecules consti-
tuting the donor has been found in favor of the dimer
half, PL, bound to protein subunit L. Investigation of a
series of mutants showed that the asymmetry is mainly
caused by different hydrogen bonding of the dimer
halves, P
L and PM, to surrounding amino acid residues
and that this asymmetry can be shifted by changing
the number of hydrogen bonds to P L or PM (Rautter
et al. 1995; Artz et al. 1997). Furthermore, a weak
correlation between the spin density on PL and the rate
of reduction of P+.
865 by soluble cytochromec2 has been
found (Rautter et al. 1996).
For the forward electron transfer reactions knowl-
edge of the spin density distribution of the lowest
unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) is important.

e.g. the charge transfer character of the excited state,
P

techniques. However, information about orbital coef-
ﬁcients of the HOMO and the LUMO can be obtained
from the triplet state,

Discussion
3BChl a
The data measured here are the ﬁrst proton hyperﬁne
data for 3BChl a. An assignment of AZ(1) and AZ(2)
in 3BChl a can be achieved by comparison with the
results for the cation radical and anion radical of BChl
a. In the Hückel MO approximation the normalized
π-spin density ρT(Ci) at a carbon position i is given
by
ρT(Ci)= 1
2[ρC(Ci)+ρA(Ci)] (1)
whereρC andρA are the spin densities of the cation
and anion radical (Carrington and McLachlan 1969).
In this approximation the spin density distribution of
the HOMO and LUMO of the triplet state are those of
the cation and anion radical, respectively. Assuming
similar Q factors for corresponding molecular posi-
tions in the cation, the anion, and the triplet state,
Equation (1) can be used to estimate the isotropic hy-
perﬁne couplings of the triplet state from those of the
cation and anion radical.
A comparison of the hyperﬁne couplings of the
cation and anion radical of BChla (see Table 1) shows
one important difference: in the cation radical there
are only large positive hyperﬁne couplings from the
β-protons at positions 7, 8, 17, 18 and from the CH
3
protons at 21 and 121 (see Scheme 1). The α-protons
at positions 5,10 and 20 have only small positive cou-
plings due to small negative π-spin densities at these
carbon positions resulting from spin polarization from
the neighboring carbon atoms (Lubitz 1991; Lubitz
et al. 1997). In contrast, the anion radical exhibits
large negative hyperﬁne couplings for the α-protons
at positions 5,10 and 20 due to the large positive car-
bonπ-spin densities at the adjacent carbon positions
(Lubitz et al. 1981; Lubitz 1991). Therefore, the neg-
ative coupling A
Z(2) of 3BChl a is assigned to the
α-protons 5, 10 and/or 20. This coupling is indicative
of spin density in the LUMO since only the anion rad-
ical (where the unpaired spin is in the LUMO) exhibits
large spin densities at these positions.
The ENDOR line 2 corresponding to AZ(2) of
3BChl a is rather broad and asymmetric (see Figure 3).
This could result from the contributions of several
nuclei (protons 5, 10 and 20) having hyperﬁne cou-
plings of similar magnitudes, as is expected from a
comparison with the BChl a anion radical (Lubitz et
al. 1981). Alternatively (or in addition) the observed
line broadening may result from the remaining ori-
entational distribution of the
3BChl a molecules, i.e.
from orientations where the magnetic ﬁeld B lies in the
molecular Z-X plane. The observed value for AZ(2),
–9.5 MHz, assigned to one or more of the methine
protons, is surprisingly large. Since the z-axis of a
planar π–π∗triplet state is perpendicular to the π
plane, AZ(2) is expected to be the out-of-plane prin-
cipal value of the hyperﬁne tensor of one or more of
the methine protons. For an isolated C–H fragment
this A
Z-value is very close to its isotropic hyperﬁne
coupling constant ( aiso) (Carrington and McLachlan
1969). For the triplet state 3BChl a the values for aiso
can be estimated from those of the BChl a cation and
anion radical. When using the aiso values for the me-
thine protons from the anion radical, BChl a−. (−6.2
to−9.7 MHz, see Table 1) and considering the fact
that in the cation radical the respective couplings are
close to zero, values between –3.1 and –4.9 MHz are
expected from Equation (1).
The assignment of the large positive coupling
AZ(1) in 3BChl a is less straight forward. It could orig-
inate from the CH 3 protons at 2 1 and/or 121,w h i c h
exhibit large positive couplings both in the cation
and in the anion radical. In addition, there could be
also contributions from the β-protons 7, 8, 17, 18,
which have large couplings only in the cation radi-
cal, and are therefore indicative of spin density in the
HOMO. Both, the CH
3 and the β-protons 7, 8, 17,
18, are expected to have small hyperﬁne anisotropies.
The observed value A
Z(1) should therefore be close
to the isotropic hyperﬁne coupling constant. Better
spectral resolution and a more detailed assignment are
expected from future single crystal experiments.
3P865

the charge transfer character of the dimeric triplet
state, 3P865, have been based so far mainly on the
evaluation of triplet- minus singlet-optical difference
spectra (Scherer and Fischer 1987; Vrieze and Hoff
1996) or on EPR experiments. Detailed data were
obtained from the determination of the zero-ﬁeld split-

single crystals of Rb. sphaeroides. The triplet z-axis
of 3P865 was found to agree within 5 degrees with
the normal vector on the average plane of P L and PM
and contributions of triplet excitation delocalisation,
55% 3PLPM, 32% PL3PM, and of charge transfer, 13%

Based on the same arguments as for 3BChl a,t h e

and 4 in

Triplet excitation delocalization and charge transfer
The triplet state, 3P865, can be described by a lin-
ear combination of two local excitations, 3PLPM and
PL3PM, and two charge transfer states, P L+ PM−and
PL−PM+ . In frozen solution with randomly oriented
RCs an experimental discrimination between the hy-
perﬁne couplings from P L or P M is not possible.
Therefore, based on the above assignment to spin den-
sities in the HOMO and LUMO of3P865, two possible
assignments remain:
(A) The largest positive and largest negative coupling
(AZ(1) and AZ(4)) of 3P865 are assigned to the
same dimer half. In this case, this dimer half
has 60% of the hyperﬁne couplings (and corre-
sponding spin densities) in the HOMO and in the
LUMO. This corresponds to 60% of local excita-
tion on this side. The other dimer half then has
40% of the spin density in the HOMO and in the
LUMO. Consequently the contribution of charge
transfer (P
L+ PM−, or vice versa) is zero.
(B) The largest positive and the smallest negative cou-
pling (AZ(1) and AZ(3)) of 3P865 are assigned to
one dimer half (P L) and the smallest positive and
largest negative coupling to the other half (P M).
In this case, each dimer half carries 40% of local

The interpretation of the primary donor triplet
state, 3P865, presented here is based on: i) the determi-
nation of the signs of the observed hyperﬁne couplings
of 3BChl a and 3P865 (relative to the sign of D), which
allows us to assign the methine proton couplings and
to relate them to spin densities in the LUMO, and ii)
the observed reduction of the values and doubling of
the number of hyperﬁne couplings in

density distribution in the HOMO and the LUMO.
However, the data do not allow to decide between
the two assignments A and B. Further experiments on
3P865 in RC single crystals are underway to determine
the magnitude and orientation of the hyperﬁne tensors
of 3P865. This will allow us to distinguish experimen-
tally between hyperﬁne tensors that belong to P L and
PM and thereby yield reliable assignments. Further-
more, we expect to obtain a more complete set of
hyperﬁne tensors from the different nuclei in 3P865.
