The radical cations of [15N]bacteriochlorophyll a and of the primary donor Psmin 15N-labeledreaction centers
of Rhodobacter sphaeroides R-26.1 were investigated in frozen solutions using two-dimensional ESEEM
methods. Both three-pulse (stimulated echo) and four-pulse (HYSCORE) sequences were employed to avoid
ambiguities in data analysis. Computer simulations of the experimental powder spectra were performed,
yielding a complete set of nitrogen hyperfine coupling tensors for both systems. The obtained tensor values
are compared with those from ENDOR measurements and from semiempirical INDO-type MO calculations.
The results obtained from 2D stimulated echo ESEEM and HYSCORE for are interpreted in terms of an
asymmetric spin density distribution over the halves of the bacteriochlorophyll dimer (‘special pair’) in the
reaction center with a ratio of approximately 5:1. This asymmetry is considerably larger at low temperatures
in the frozen state than at room temperature. It is postulated that two different conformational states of the
dimer exist at these temperatures with different spin density distributions.
1. Introduction
In photosynthetic purple bacteria, reaction centers (RC’s)
mediate the conversion of light into chemical energy by light-
induced charge separation. In this process an electron is
transferred from the photoexcited primary electron donor P
(labeled D in some references), a dimer of bacteriochlorophyll
(BChl) molecules, via intermediate electron acceptorsto a first
and a second quinone which are both coupled to a non-heme
Fe2+. The cofactors are rigidly held in a protein matrix made
up of three subunits (L, M, and H). Pigments, quinones, the
metal, and the protein are all involved in the highly efficient
electron transfer process,the details of which are not yet fully
understood (for recent reviews see refs 1—3).
Reaction centersfrom two purple bacteria, Rhodopseudomo-
nas(Rp.)viridis and Rhodobacter(Rb.)sphaeroides, have been
crystallized, and their structures were solved by X-ray crystal-
lography.4-5 This provides the spatial arrangement of the
cofactors and the protein for the speciesRb. sphaeroideswhich
is studied here.5 To understand the function of the RC from
first principles, a knowledge of both the spatial and the
electronic structure of the reactants is required. Information
about the latter can be obtained from spectroscopic techniques.
The method used to explore the electronic structure of the
radical ions formed in the chargeseparationprocessis hyperfine
spectroscopy. This technique measures the electron—nuclear
hyperfine coupling constants (hfc’s) which—after assignment
to specific nuclei—yield a map of the distribution q of the
unpairedelectron spin over the molecule. Electron paramagnetic
resonance (EPR) and, in particular, electron nuclear double
resonance(ENDOR) have been extensively used to investigate
the structuresof the monomeric BChl a'+and the primary donor
cation radical P*+in bacterial RC’s (reviewed in ref 6). It could
be shown from measurementson RC single crystals that the
two bacteriochlorophylls form a supermolecule with the elec-

4.2. Discussion
Severalinvestigationswere undertakenin the past with the
aim to determinenitrogen hfc tensorsin BChl a'+. Thereby
the 15Nhfc tensor principal values listed in Table 2 were
obtainedfrom cw-ENDOR andESEEM experimentsperformed
in frozen solutions.
In the primary echo and the stimulated echo ESEEM
experiments,15autoregressionandFourier transformationmeth-
ods were usedto obtain frequency spectraof the time domain
signals. Only four different r values were employed in the
stimulatedecho experimentspresentedin ref 15, which led to
very limited information;i.e.,only two hfc valueswereobtained.
Furthermore, in this paper the peak positions were directly
assigned to the Ax hfc tensor components without further
simulation. As waspointedout later by Lin et al.16this is rather
questionable becausepeak positions in ESEEM spectra of
powder samplesdo not directly yield the principal values of
thehfc tensors. Theseauthorsperformedimprovedexperiments
in which ID stimulatedecho traces,employing 23 different r
values,were measuredto avoid the blind spotsin the spectra.
Simulationsof the signalsin the time domain were performed
using four axially symmetric hfc tensors.16
Astashkinet al.18usednonaxial hfc tensorsin the simulation
of theirspectraandtherebyobtainedabetteragreementbetween
experiment and simulation. Their tensors showed quite pro-
nounceddeviationsfrom axial symmetry. The most important
results were obtained from evaluation of primary echo
ESEEM.52 The experimentally (ESEEM, ENDOR) and theo-
retically (RHF-INDO/SP) determinedhfc tensor values given
in this work clearly show a smaller degreeof nonaxiality than
thosefound in ref 18. We tried to simulateour stimulatedecho
ESEEM employing the hfc’s from this referenceand obtained
significant deviations in the ratio of peak intensities although
the positions of the lines were still acceptable.53
In the caseof the [15N]BChla radical cation, 15NENDOR
allows an independentdeterminationof the hfc tensorvalues.

6. Conclusions
In this paper three-pulse (stimulated echo) and four-pulse
(HYSCORE) ESEEM methodshavebeenapplied to elucidate
the nitrogen hfc’s in complex biological systems in frozen
solutions. The systemsinvestigatedare the radical cations of
the monomeric [15N]BChl a and of the primary donor Pg65in
fully I5N-labeledbacterial RC’s of Rb.sphaeroidesR 26.1. It
could be shown that a complete set of 15Nhfc tensorscan be
obtained by application of both ENDOR and ESEEM tech-
niques. In particular,a substantialgain in resolutionis obtained
in the HYSCORE experiment,which allows the separationof
overlapping powder patternsin the two-dimensional spectrum.
Thereby it is demonstratedthat thesemethodsare able to deal
even with complicated immobilized radicals like the primary
donor containingeight magnetically inequivalent nitrogen
nuclei.
Pjg5had already been well characterizedby cw-EPR and
ENDOR spectroscopyin RC single crystals. It is thereforean
ideal systemto prove the potential of thesemodempulsedEPR
techniquesfor future applications on other biological systems
which can not be crystallized yet.
From the measuredand assigned15Nhfc’s in Pjgj in frozen
RC’s, a spin density ratio of approximately 5:1 was deduced.
This contrastswith theresultsobtainedfrom ENDOR nearroom
temperature,which gave a ratio of only 2:1. Obviously there
exist two distinct conformational statesof the primary donor
Pjg5. A possible explanation could lie in a change of the
spatialand/orelectronicstructureof thedimer andits surround-
ing upon freezing the RC. The reasonsfor the existence of
these statesand the possible functional relevanceare not yet
understood and require detailed future investigations. The
changeof the electronic structure of the dimer observedhere
might also be of importance for other spectroscopicstudies
performed on RC’s at low temperatures.
