Abstract 
The hyperfine couplings of the nitrogen nuclei in the radical cations of both 15N-labelled chlorophyll a and the primary donor 
P700 in Photosystem | of Synechococcus elongatus and spinach (Spinacea oleracea) in frozen solutions were investigated 
by ENDOR and, for confirmation, by two-dimensional ESEEM techniques. In addition, t H ENDOR experiments were 
performed on these compounds. The experimental ~SN hypeffine couplings of the chlorophyll a radical cation are compared 
with theoretical ones obtained by RHF-INDO/SP calculations and with the respective hyperfine couplings in the closely 
related ~SN-bacteriochlorophyll a radical cation. Based on the observed ~SN and 1n hyperfine couplings two possible models 
are discussed for P~-0b: (a) the special pair model with a strongly asymmetric spin density distribution over the dimer halves; 
(b) the model of a strongly perturbed chlorophyll a monomer.

electron is delocalized over the halves of a dimer con- 
sisting of two Chl a molecules, forming the primary 
donor PT00. Subsequently; several other l H ENDOR 
experiments were performed on P+0o [ 15-17]. In con-

was concluded by O'Malley and Babcock [ 16] that 
the 1H ENDOR spectrum of P+0o could be assigned 
to a monomeric Chl a radical cation perturbed by the

phyll doublet ground state was assumed to be modi- 
fied by mixing with the first excited doublet state in- 
duced by interaction with the surroundings. However, 
the most recent IH ENDOR results [ 18 ] on P+oo were 
interpreted again in terms of a strongly asymmetric 
spin density distribution with a ratio of about 3 : 1 over 
the halves of a special pair of two Chl a molecules. 
The frozen solution IH-ENDOR spectra of P+~o in PSI 
exhibited several broad lines which are due to methyl 
proton hfc's. They were tentatively assigned to spe- 
cific protons in the Chl a molecule(s) forming P700 
[14-18]. 
The recent availability of large PSI single crystals 
[ 8] allowed us to determine the full l H hfc tensors 
of three methyl groups using ENDOR spectroscopy 
[ 19,20]. A preliminary analysis of these data was also 
attempted by assuming that P+o0 consists of a chloro- 
phyll dimer, in analogy to the special pair model of the 
primary donor P~65 in bacterial photosynthesis [ 7,21 ]. 
The resulting asymmetry ratio of the spin density dis- 
tribution was about 4 : 1 [ 19,20]. Based on this work 
and on a comparison with the monomeric Chl a +" [ 22 ] 
essential parts of the frozen solution I H-ENDOR spec- 
trum of P~-o0 can now be interpreted in a reliable way. 
The evaluation of the 1H hyperfine spectrum suf- 
fers from poor resolution due to the large number of 
hydrogen nuclei coupled to the unpaired electron spin 
in P~-0o leading to many overlapping hyperfine lines. 
An alternative way to probe the spin density distribu- 
tion in P~-oo is the determination of the nitrogen hfc's. 
Chl a contains only four nitrogen nuclei. Therefore 
an assumed Chl a dimer will exhibit a maximum of 
eight different nitrogen hfc's. The resulting hyperfine 
spectrum might be better resolved and easier to inter- 
pret than the respective l H hyperfine spectrum. So far 
the observation of 14N-ENDOR has been reported nei- 
ther for Chl a +" nor for P~-oo. This is due to the small 
magnetic moment of the 14N nucleus and the resulting 
low sensitivity of ENDOR for its hyperfine transitions 
[ 23 ]. In addition the quadrupole moment of the nat- 
ural abundant 14N (I = l) leads to complications in 
the interpretation of the experimental spectra. 
The ESEEM technique (for reviews see Refs. 
[24,25] ) circumvents the intrinsic sensitivity prob- 
lem of cw-ENDOR. Thus, several ESEEM experi- 
ments have been performed in the past in order to 
determine the nitrogen hfc's in P~-o0 [26-28]. How- 
ever, the quadrupole moment of 14N still led to quite 
complicated spectra. Therefore, in order to reduce 
ambiguities in the evaluation of the spectra, 15N 
(I = 1/2) labeled monomeric Chl a +" and P~-0o have 
also been investigated [ 29,30]. All these experiments 
are reviewed in Ref. [25]. However, from these data 
no final conclusion concerning the monomeric or 
dimeric nature of P~-o0 could be drawn [25]. The lat- 
est attempt to estimate the asymmetry ratio of the spin 
density distribution over the dimer halves was under- 
taken by Davis et al. [ 28 ]. These authors reported a

2. Experimental 
2.1. Samples 
15N-PS l from spinach. Spinach chloroplasts, grown 
hydroponically on a medium containing KI5NO3 
(98%), were suspended in the dark at 0°C at 2.5 mg 
Chl/ml in 20 mM MES-NaOH, 5 mM MgCI2, 15 mM 
NaCI for 60 min. They were then treated in darkness 
at 0°C with 5% Triton X-100 for 30 min. After cen- 
trifugation (30 min at 40000 g) the supernatant, con- 
talning the solubilized PS I, was diluted with 50 mM 
Tris-HCl/Triton X-100, pH 7.8 to a detergent/Chl 
ratio of 75/1 (w/w). An amount of the material cor- 
responding to about 30 mg chlorophyll was applied 
to a hydroxylapatite column, pre-equilibrated with 10 
mM Na-phosphate, pH 7.0 and eluted according to 
Ref. [ 34]. The eluate was collected and concentrated 
with Centriprep 30 tubes (Centricon) at 5000 g. The 
PSI preparation had a Chl a/Chl b ratio of 19 and 
Chl/P700 (determined from the difference spectrum 
of ferricyanide-oxidized minus ascorbate-reduced 
samples) ratio of 33. Illumination of the preparation 
at 77 K resulted in oxidation of P700 and light-induced 
reduction of iron-sulfur centers A and B, identified 
by their respective EPR signals (e.g. see Ref. [3] ). 
The final preparation used in the experiments con- 
tained about 3 mg/ml Chl, the PS I concentration was 
approximately 90/zM in P700. 
15N-PS l from Synechococcus elongatus. PSI was 
prepared from 15N labelled Synechococcus elongatus 
cells (1or details see Ref.. [35]). Triton X-100 was 
used as detergent for solubilizing the PSI complex. 
For further purification chromatography on DEAE 
and sucrose gradient centrifugation was employed. 
The procedure essentially followed that described in 
Ref. [36]. The final Chl concentration was about 0.6 
mg/ml corresponding to a concentration of about 20 
/xM in PT00. 
The PS I particles were filled in quartz capillaries 
(o.d. 4 mm, i.d. 3 mm) and illuminated for 1 minute 
at ambient temperature employing a 1000 W tung- 
sten projection lamp equipped with a waterfilter of 10 
cm pathlength to yield the radical cation P~-00. Subse- 
quently the samples were quickly frozen under illumi- 
nation in liquid nitrogen. 
15N-Chl a. The 15N-Chi a was extracted with ace- 
tone from 15N labelled spinach and precipitated with 
dioxane/water (peroxide and oxygen free) as de- 
scribed in Ref. [37]. Chl a was separated from Chl b 
and carotenoids on an ion-exchange column (DEAE 
Sepharose CL-6B) [38]. The final purification was 
done by preparative HPLC using a reversed-phase C- 
18 column (5/zm Nucleosil, Knauer), run with pure 
methanol at a rate of 7.5 ml/min. The Chl a was eluted 
after approximately 12 min. The purity was checked 
by optical and NMR spectroscopy [39]. 15N-Chl a +" 
was generated on a high vacuum line by oxidation of 
a 5 x 10 -4 M solution of 15N-Chl a in CH2C12/THF 
10 : 1 (v/v) with a 10 fold excess of iodine dissolved 
in degassed CH2C12. A stoichiometric equivalent of 
AgCIO4 was added [40] to the iodine to shift the 
redox equilibrium of the reaction versus oxidation of 
the Chl a. Finally the sample was quickly frozen in 
liquid nitrogen to prevent degradation of the radical 
cation and transferred to the spectrometer.

CF 935 (helium gas flow system). All experiments 
were performed on the center of the EPR absorption

also yield 15N hfc tensors which are very close to ax-

essentially axial hfc tensor symmetry is reasonable for 
15N-Chl a +" and also for 15N-P~-~0. Further evidence 
can be drawn from the analogy to the primary donor 
radical cation P~65 in the bacterial RC which also ex-

4. Conclusions 
Comparison of the 1H-ENDOR results for the three 
assigned methyl proton hfc's ofChl a +' and P+00 shows 
that in P+0o the average overall spin density derived 
from these hfc's is reduced by about 15%. The promi- 
nent A± nitrogen hfc's of 15N-Chl a +" determined 
by 15N-ENDOR are, however, comparable to those of 
~sN-P~-~0. It is noteworthy that at least one Air 15N hfc 
is larger in P~-00 than in Chl a +'. An increase has also 
been found for the isotropic proton hfc of the methyl 
group at position 7 [ 19,20]. 
These results can be rationalized in terms of a dimer 
model for P~-00 if one assumes a strongly asymmet- 
ric spin density distribution over the Chl a halves. 
It is possible that some minor rearrangements of the 
spin density distribution between different parts of the 
macrocycle occur in addition to the expected overall 
reduction, when going from monomeric Chl a +" to the 
Chl a molecule forming one half in the dimer P+00. 
In this case the scaling factors of the proton and the 
nitrogen hfc's could be slightly different. Some evi- 
dence for such an effect exists in the analogous case 
of the 15N hfc's present in the primary donor 15N-P~-65 
of purple bacteria [21,60]. 
The occurrence of some additional smaller 15N hfc's 
in 15N-P~-~0, which are not present in 15N-Chl a +', is 
particularly important. This observation already made 
in the 15N-ENDOR spectra is clearly confirmed by 
the 2D HYSCORE experiments in which these hfc's 
could be separated from the large ones. It is consistent 
with the model of PT00 being a dimer of two Chl a 
molecules which shows a Strongly asymmetric spin 
density distribution 5 over the dimer halves in the rad- 
ical cation P~-00. 
In conclusion, the results from 15N-ENDOR and 
15N-ESEEM spectroscopy agree with the interpreta-

for P~-00. Especially the occurrence of the small nitro- 
gen hfc's can be taken as good evidence in favor of 
this model. However, it cannot be excluded that these
